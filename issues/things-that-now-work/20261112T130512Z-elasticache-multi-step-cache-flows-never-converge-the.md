Cause 1 of 2 — a replication group cannot be scaled and failed over inside a client's budget, so the data consequence of a primary-role move is never observable. Failing check: elasticache.e2e-cluster-scale-failover-data-consequence.
AWS: Smithy (services/service-com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9/smithy-model/com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9.json) — IncreaseReplicaCount(ReplicationGroupId required, NewReplicaCount, ApplyImmediately required) returns ReplicationGroup and models InvalidReplicationGroupStateFault {InvalidReplicationGroupState,400}, InvalidCacheClusterStateFault {InvalidCacheClusterState,400}, NodeQuotaForCustomerExceededFault {400}, ReplicationGroupNotFoundFault {ReplicationGroupNotFoundFault,404}; TestFailover(ReplicationGroupId required, NodeGroupId required, @length 1..4, @pattern ^\d+$) returns ReplicationGroup and models TestFailoverNotAvailableFault {400}, InvalidReplicationGroupStateFault {400}, NodeGroupNotFoundFault {404}, APICallRateForCustomerExceededFault {400}; DescribeReplicationGroups returns ReplicationGroups and models ReplicationGroupNotFoundFault {404}. NodeGroup$Status is documented "creating, available, modifying, deleting"; NodeGroup$PrimaryEndpoint is "The endpoint of the primary node in this node group (shard)"; NodeGroupMember carries CacheClusterId, CacheNodeId and CurrentRole "primary or replica"; Endpoint{Address "The DNS hostname of the cache node", Port "The port number that the cache engine is listening on"} is "the information required for client programs to connect to a cache node". Recorded behavior for the sequencing: CreateReplicationGroup(NumCacheClusters=2, AutomaticFailoverEnabled=true) settles Status 'available'; a RESP session to NodeGroups[0].PrimaryEndpoint.{Address,Port} from inside the group's VPC answers SET with the fixed wire reply `+OK` and GET with the stored bulk string; IncreaseReplicaCount(NewReplicaCount=2, ApplyImmediately=true) moves the group to 'modifying' and back to 'available' with NodeGroups[0].NodeGroupMembers holding three members; TestFailover(NodeGroupId=<the shard's id>) moves the group through 'modifying' back to 'available' with the primary role on a DIFFERENT NodeGroupMember, and the key written before the failover is still readable at the (possibly re-pointed) PrimaryEndpoint. Group ids, hosts, ports, node-group ids and key names are per-run values; the status progressions, the member count after the scale and the survival of the written value across the failover are the requirement.
Emulator: the check is killed at the 60 s budget with NO assertion evaluated — not the pre-failover SET/GET pair, not `len(NodeGroupMembers) == 3` after the scale, not the post-failover GET. The flow chains three waits (_wait_rg_available after create, again after IncreaseReplicaCount with members>=3, again after TestFailover) on DescribeReplicationGroups at a 2 s poll plus two in-VPC RESP sessions whose failed connects are retried for 60 s each. Mechanism in this checkout: services/service-com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9/src/state.rs:406 `pub const CLUSTER_TRANSITION: Duration = Duration::from_millis(500);` with the doc comment at :396-405 — "it is deliberately NOT zero, so a caller acting straight after the call still observes the transient status and the state errors that go with it. The due instant is stored on the record and materialised by whichever later call reads it — nothing sleeps" — so a client's first poll after each create/modify/failover observes the transient status and sleeps its interval even though the emulator settled in 500 ms. Hypothesis: the dominant cost here is the RESP sessions rather than the polls, because the advertised PrimaryEndpoint accepts no TCP from inside the VPC (the sibling data-plane checks of this same replay, owned by 20261111T005655Z, report the emulator failing to open that connection) and `_resp` retries a `connect:` failure for a full 60 s window; the two contributions cannot be separated from this output because no assertion fired.
Why: a client relying on AWS's behavior scales a shard and then exercises TestFailover precisely to prove that a primary-role move does not lose data. Against this emulator none of that is observable: the client cannot confirm the replica was added, cannot confirm the failover completed, and cannot read back a key it wrote before the failover. A run that dies at the budget is indistinguishable from a group that never settles, from a failover that never happens, and from an engine that silently drops the keyspace, so a failover-resilience procedure validated against the emulator proves nothing.
Fix: two components — (a) the status-settling path of the elasticache crate's state (state.rs settle/transition helpers around CLUSTER_TRANSITION), so a record whose transition instant has already passed reads back SETTLED to the FIRST call that observes it while the create/modify/TestFailover RESPONSE keeps reporting the transient status other checks assert; and (b) the IncreaseReplicaCount / TestFailover handlers together with the ReplicationGroup projection and the replication-group data plane, so NodeGroupMembers really grows to the requested count, CurrentRole really moves to another member, and the emulator serves RESP on the Address:Port its NodeGroups[].PrimaryEndpoint publishes with the pre-failover keyspace intact.
Traces: elasticache.e2e-cluster-scale-failover-data-consequence; mechanism citation services/service-com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9/src/state.rs:406 with the comment at :396-405.

Cause 2 of 2 — the serverless snapshot/restore round trip never converges, so restored data is never observable. Failing check: elasticache.e2e-serverless-snapshot-restore-flow.
AWS: Smithy — CreateServerlessCache(ServerlessCacheName required, Engine required, SecurityGroupIds, SubnetIds, SnapshotArnsToRestore) returns ServerlessCache and models InvalidParameterValueException {InvalidParameterValue,400}, InvalidParameterCombinationException {InvalidParameterCombination,400}, ServerlessCacheAlreadyExistsFault {400}, ServerlessCacheNotFoundFault {ServerlessCacheNotFoundFault,404}, ServerlessCacheQuotaForCustomerExceededFault {400}; CreateServerlessCacheSnapshot(ServerlessCacheSnapshotName required, ServerlessCacheName required) returns ServerlessCacheSnapshot and models InvalidServerlessCacheStateFault {400}, ServerlessCacheNotFoundFault {404}, ServerlessCacheSnapshotAlreadyExistsFault {400}; DescribeServerlessCaches / DescribeServerlessCacheSnapshots return the lists and model ServerlessCacheNotFoundFault {404}. ServerlessCache$Status is documented "The allowed values are CREATING, AVAILABLE, DELETING, CREATE-FAILED and MODIFYING" (the recorded wire values are lower case), and ServerlessCache$Endpoint is an Endpoint{Address,Port} — "the information required for client programs to connect to a cache node". Recorded behavior: CreateServerlessCache settles DescribeServerlessCaches Status 'available' with an Endpoint; a TLS RESP session to that Address:Port from inside the cache's VPC answers SET with the fixed reply `+OK` and GET with the stored bulk string; CreateServerlessCacheSnapshot settles Status 'available' and publishes an ARN; DeleteServerlessCache removes the cache (DescribeServerlessCaches then answers 404 ServerlessCacheNotFoundFault); CreateServerlessCache(SnapshotArnsToRestore=[that ARN]) settles 'available' with the snapshot's keyspace loaded, so a GET of the pre-snapshot key on the NEW cache returns the original value. Cache names, snapshot names, ARNs, hosts and ports are per-run values; the status progressions and the persistence of the pre-snapshot value across the restore are the requirement.
Emulator: the check is killed at the 60 s budget with NO assertion evaluated — neither the first SET/GET pair on cache 1 nor the restored GET on cache 2. The flow chains four waits (cache 1 available at a 2 s poll, snapshot available at 2 s, cache 1 gone at a 15 s poll, cache 2 available at 2 s) plus two in-VPC TLS RESP sessions. Mechanism: the same first-poll-never-settles behavior cited in Cause 1 (state.rs:406 CLUSTER_TRANSITION, :416 SNAPSHOT_TRANSITION, both 500 ms, materialised only by a later read) makes each wait cost the client its whole interval, and the post-delete wait in `_delete_serverless_cache_if_present` sleeps 15 s per probe. Hypothesis: the TLS sessions against an endpoint that accepts no TCP add the remainder; the contributions cannot be separated here because no assertion fired, and whether the restore actually loads the snapshot's keyspace is therefore also unverified.
Why: snapshot-and-restore is the only way an ElastiCache Serverless client moves or recovers a dataset. Against this emulator a client cannot confirm that a serverless cache became available, that a snapshot completed, that the old cache was really deleted, or that the restored cache contains the pre-snapshot data; a timed-out run is indistinguishable from a snapshot that never settles and from a restore that silently produces an empty cache, so a backup/restore runbook validated against the emulator proves nothing. SDK waiters and CI pipelines simply time out.
Fix: two components — (a) the status-settling path of the elasticache crate's state (same component as Cause 1, covering CLUSTER_TRANSITION and SNAPSHOT_TRANSITION), so DescribeServerlessCaches / DescribeServerlessCacheSnapshots report the settled status to the client's FIRST poll once due and a deleted cache is absent (404 ServerlessCacheNotFoundFault) on the first poll after its deletion is due; and (b) the CreateServerlessCache restore path (SnapshotArnsToRestore) plus the serverless data plane, so the emulator serves RESP on the Address:Port its ServerlessCache$Endpoint projection publishes and the restored cache really holds the snapshot's keyspace.
