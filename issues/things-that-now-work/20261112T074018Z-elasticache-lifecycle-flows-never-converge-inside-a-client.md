Cause 1 of 2 — the single-node snapshot create / copy / delete / cluster-delete round trip never converges. Failing check: elasticache.snapshot-of-a-single-node-cluster-copies-and-deletes.
AWS: Smithy (services/service-com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9/smithy-model/com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9.json) — Snapshot$SnapshotStatus is documented "creating | available | restoring | copying | deleting"; CreateSnapshot returns Snapshot and models CacheClusterNotFoundFault (404 CacheClusterNotFound), InvalidCacheClusterStateFault (400 InvalidCacheClusterState), SnapshotAlreadyExistsFault (400) and SnapshotFeatureNotSupportedFault (400); CopySnapshot returns Snapshot and models SnapshotNotFoundFault (404 SnapshotNotFoundFault) and InvalidSnapshotStateFault (400 InvalidSnapshotState); DeleteSnapshot returns Snapshot and models SnapshotNotFoundFault (404) and InvalidSnapshotStateFault (400). Recorded behavior for the sequencing: CreateSnapshot of a single-node redis cluster reports `creating` and then settles `available` carrying exactly one NodeSnapshot and the source CacheClusterId; CopySnapshot yields an INDEPENDENT snapshot that reports creating/copying/available, records the SOURCE CacheClusterId and one NodeSnapshot, and survives DeleteSnapshot of its source; DeleteSnapshot then removes each snapshot (DescribeSnapshots answers 404 SnapshotNotFoundFault / an empty list) and DeleteCacheCluster removes the cluster (DescribeCacheClusters then answers 404 CacheClusterNotFound). Snapshot names, cluster ids and timestamps are per-run values, the status progression and the independence of the copy are the requirement.
Emulator: the check is killed at the 60 s budget with NO assertion evaluated — not the one-NodeSnapshot assertion, not the copy's CacheClusterId, not the coexistence of both snapshots, not the survivor remaining `available`, not the final absences. The flow chains six sequential waits (cluster available, source snapshot available, copy available, source snapshot gone, copy gone, cluster gone) at interval_s=15, so a client that misses each wait on its first poll spends 90 s of sleep. Mechanism in this checkout: services/service-com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9/src/state.rs:406 `pub const CLUSTER_TRANSITION: Duration = Duration::from_millis(500);` and :416 `SNAPSHOT_TRANSITION` likewise 500 ms, documented at :396-415 as "deliberately NOT zero, so a caller acting straight after the call still observes the transient status ... The due instant is stored on the record and materialised by whichever later call reads it — nothing sleeps". So a resource the emulator settled 500 ms after the call is still reported transient to the first poll, which then sleeps its whole interval. Hypothesis: that per-wait interval loss, chained six times, is the whole cost; the alternative — that one specific status (e.g. the copy's `available`, or the post-delete absence) is never reached at all — cannot be separated from this output because no assertion fired.
Why: a client written against AWS polls SnapshotStatus on an AWS-sized interval. Against this emulator every wait costs that full interval instead of returning on the first poll once the transition is due, so a snapshot-then-copy-then-two-deletes flow takes minutes and is indistinguishable from a snapshot that never settles or a delete that never completes; SDK waiters and CI pipelines time out, and nothing about the copy's independence from its source is observable.
Fix: the status-settling path of the elasticache crate's state (state.rs settle/transition helpers around CLUSTER_TRANSITION / SNAPSHOT_TRANSITION) — a record whose transition instant has already passed must read back SETTLED to the FIRST call that observes it, while the create/copy/delete RESPONSE keeps reporting the transient status other checks assert; then the DescribeSnapshots / DescribeCacheClusters projections and the CopySnapshot / DeleteSnapshot handlers must carry the flow to its end (one NodeSnapshot per node, source CacheClusterId on the copy, copy unaffected by the source's deletion).

Cause 2 of 2 — RBAC revocation is never observable: the user-group remove/re-add flow on an attached replication group burns its whole budget in status polls and RESP sessions. Failing check: elasticache.e2e-user-group-access-revocation-flow.
AWS: Smithy — ModifyUserGroup takes UserIdsToAdd / UserIdsToRemove (UserIdListInput, @length min 1) and returns UserGroup{UserGroupId, Status, UserIds, PendingChanges, ReplicationGroups, ...}, modeling DefaultUserRequired (400), InvalidUserGroupStateFault (400 InvalidUserGroupState), UserGroupNotFoundFault (404 UserGroupNotFound) and UserNotFoundFault (404 UserNotFound); ReplicationGroup$Status is documented "creating, available, modifying, deleting, create-failed, snapshotting"; Endpoint$Address is "The DNS hostname of the cache node" and Endpoint$Port "The port number that the cache engine is listening on". Recorded behavior for the rest: ModifyUserGroup(UserIdsToRemove=[u]) moves the group to `modifying` and back to `active` WITHOUT u in UserIds, the attached replication group returns to `available`, and a RESP session to NodeGroups[0].PrimaryEndpoint.{Address,Port} from inside the group's VPC then REFUSES that user's previously-accepted AUTH with a reply whose fixed wire prefix is `-WRONGPASS` (or `-NOAUTH` when no AUTH has succeeded); ModifyUserGroup(UserIdsToAdd=[u]) settles the group `active` with u restored and makes the IDENTICAL AUTH succeed again (`+OK`). The reply prefixes are the fixed text; the user id, group id, password and endpoint host/port are per-run values from the request and the emulator's state.
Emulator: the check dies at the 60 s budget with no assertion evaluated — neither the positive control (AUTH accepted while the user is in the group), nor the refusal after removal, nor the restore after re-adding. The flow chains six waits (replication group available after create at interval 20 s, available-and-group-attached after ModifyReplicationGroup at 20 s, then group-active / replication-group-available pairs at 15 s after each ModifyUserGroup) plus three in-VPC RESP sessions. Mechanism: the same first-poll-never-settles behavior cited in Cause 1 (state.rs:406/:416) makes each of those six waits cost a client its full interval. Hypothesis: the RESP sessions add the remainder, because the advertised PrimaryEndpoint accepts no TCP from inside the VPC — the sibling data-plane checks in this same replay (owned by 20261111T005655Z) report the emulator's own failure to open that connection — and the probe retries a failed connect for its whole window; the two contributions cannot be separated here because no assertion fired.
Why: RBAC is the only mechanism by which an ElastiCache client scopes access to a replication group, and against this emulator a client cannot confirm any part of a revocation: it cannot tell that removing a user from the attached group really stopped that user from authenticating, nor that re-adding it restored access. A timed-out run is indistinguishable from a group that never settled and from an engine that authenticates everyone, so an access-revocation procedure validated against the emulator proves nothing.
Fix: two components — (a) the elasticache crate's status-settling path, so each of the six waits is satisfied by the client's first poll once due (same component as Cause 1); and (b) the ElastiCache replication-group data plane, so the emulator serves RESP on the Address:Port its NodeGroups[].PrimaryEndpoint projection publishes and enforces the attached user group's membership there (a removed user's AUTH refused with `-WRONGPASS`/`-NOAUTH`, a restored user's identical AUTH accepted with `+OK`).
Traces: elasticache.e2e-user-group-access-revocation-flow; mechanism citation services/service-com-amazonaws-elasticache-amazonelasticachev9/src/state.rs:406 and :416.
